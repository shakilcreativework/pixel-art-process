01 — MODE SELECTION — DECIDE THIS FIRST

Ask:

Does the image have genuinely empty space around the subject that is intended to remain blank, or does the artwork fill the entire frame?

MODE A — FULL-SCENE

The artwork fills the entire canvas from edge to edge.

Examples:

- Landscapes
- Full illustrated scenes
- Wallpapers
- Environments
- Scenes with sky, ground, water, buildings, or atmosphere reaching the canvas edges

The scene itself is the artwork.

→ Skip the Mode B background-color restrictions.

→ The sky, water, atmosphere, and environment may use deliberate color variation and discrete cell shading.

MODE B — ISOLATED-ASSET

Use Mode B when the subject is intended to be isolated, cropped, exported, or have its background removed later.

Examples:

- Icons
- Characters
- Objects
- Props
- Sprites
- Decorative assets
- Asset sheets containing multiple separate objects

Mode B requires strict background isolation with zero tolerance for violations.

MODE B ABSOLUTE BACKGROUND RULE

The background must:

- Use exactly ONE single, flat, uniform color
- Extend edge-to-edge
- Be selected specifically for the current design
- Be unique to the current generation whenever practical
- Be commercially attractive and suitable for Adobe Stock, Shutterstock, and other microstock marketplaces
- Have strong, clean visual separation from the actual design
- Have zero color collision with the actual design
- Never appear as a design color anywhere inside the subject

CRITICAL BACKGROUND SELECTION ORDER

First establish the complete design palette.

Then analyze the actual design colors, dominant colors, luminance, contrast, saturation, visual hierarchy, subject category, and overall mood.

Only after that, select the background color.

The background color must be chosen to complement the finished design rather than forcing the design to adapt to a predetermined background.

UNIQUE BACKGROUND COLOR RULE

Do not automatically default to white, black, gray, or another repeatedly reused background color.

For every new artwork, intentionally vary the background selection when the design allows it.

Prefer a refined, professional, commercially useful color that:

- Is clearly distinct from every design color
- Does not visually compete with the focal subject
- Provides strong subject readability
- Supports the overall composition
- Looks polished in stock-marketplace previews
- Works well for isolated assets and later background removal
- Avoids muddy or visually weak combinations
- Avoids unnecessarily fluorescent, harsh, or distracting colors

The background may be selected from a broad professional color range, including tasteful light, mid-tone, or dark hues, provided that the chosen color satisfies all separation and collision rules.

The exact background color must be intentionally determined from the current design palette, not copied blindly from a fixed default.

ZERO BACKGROUND-COLOR COLLISION
